How to 3D print cosplay armor and helmets that are bigger than your printer

Most helmets and chest pieces won't fit on a regular print bed in one go. Split them into a few pieces, join them cleanly, and nobody will see the seams once it's painted.

1. Scale it to you

Wearables have to fit a body, so measure before you scale. For a helmet, measure around your head and from chin to crown, then add room for padding. For armor, measure the body part it covers. Scale the model so its inside fits those numbers, not just its outside height, and print a cheap test ring or band first if you're unsure.

2. Split it where seams hide

  • Follow panel lines, trim edges and raised details wherever you can. Seams there disappear after paint.
  • Avoid cutting through the face, visor or logos.
  • Fewer, bigger pieces are stronger and faster to finish than lots of small ones.

3. Choose the filament

  • PLA is easy to print and sand, but softens in a hot car or strong sun.
  • PETG handles heat better and is tougher, and it's a good pick for anything worn outside.
  • Two or three walls and low infill keep pieces light enough to wear all day.

4. Join the pieces

Alignment pegs make the pieces sit in the same place every time, then glue holds them. CA glue sets fast; epoxy fills gaps and is stronger. On the inside of a helmet, a strip of fiberglass cloth or a printed reinforcing plate over each seam adds a lot of strength.

5. Finish and wear

  • Fill seams with filler or body putty, sand, then use filler primer until the layer lines are gone.
  • Paint, then seal with a clear coat.
  • Add foam padding inside, and straps or magnets to hold armor on.

Questions

Can I 3D print a full-size helmet on a small printer?

Yes. Split it into pieces that fit your bed, join them with pegs and glue, then fill and sand the seams before painting.

What's the best filament for cosplay armor?

PETG if it will be worn outside or left in a car, since PLA softens in heat. PLA is fine for indoor use and is easier to sand.

How do I hide the seams on split prints?

Cut along panel lines or edges where possible, then fill the seams with filler or body putty, sand, and use filler primer before painting.
